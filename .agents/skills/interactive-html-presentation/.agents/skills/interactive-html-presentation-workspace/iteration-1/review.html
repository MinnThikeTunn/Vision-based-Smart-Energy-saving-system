Act II \u00c2\u00b7 The Scaling Factor
\n
Preventing Saturation
\n

Why Divide by $\\sqrt{d_k}$?

\n
\n
\n
\n
\n For large values of $d_k$ (dimension size), dot products grow extremely large in magnitude. This pushes the softmax function into regions with extremely small gradients.\n
\n
\n $$\\text{Softmax}(x)_i = \\frac{e^{x_i}}{\\sum_j e^{x_j}}$$\n
\n

\n By scaling by $\\frac{1}{\\sqrt{d_k}}$, we maintain the variance of the dot products at $1.0$, preventing the softmax from saturating (which would otherwise result in flat gradients and dead learning).\n

\n
\n
\n

The Gradient Killer

\n
    \n
  • Assume components of $q$ and $k$ are independent random variables with mean $0$ and variance $1$.
    • \n
  • Their dot product $q \\cdot k = \\sum_{i=1}^{d_k} q_i k_i$ has mean $0$ and variance $d_k$.
    • \n
  • Dividing by $\\sqrt{d_k}$ pulls the variance back to $1.0$.
