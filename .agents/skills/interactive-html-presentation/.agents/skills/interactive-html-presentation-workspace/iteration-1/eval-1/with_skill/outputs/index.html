Act I · Mathematics

The Update Formula

The parameters are iteratively adjusted by taking small steps opposite to the gradient vector of the loss function.
Optimal steps yield convergence; poor tuning yields divergence.
Core Equation

Iterative Update Rule

$$\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta \nabla L(\theta_t)$$

Where $\theta$ represents the weights, $\eta$ is the learning rate, and $\nabla L(\theta)$ is the slope/gradient of the loss function. We subtract the gradient to step downhill toward the minimum.

Act III · Analysis

Learning Rate Regimes

The choice of learning rate $\eta$ dramatically determines the outcome of gradient descent.
Slow Convergence

$\eta < 0.5$

Steps are tiny and proceed in a straight line toward the minimum. Highly stable but computationally slow.

Perfect Optimization

$\eta = 0.5$

The step reaches the absolute minimum at $x = 0$ in exactly one iteration. The optimal scenario.

Oscillating Steps

$0.5 < \eta < 1.0$

Steps overshoot the minimum, bouncing back and forth across the valley, but still successfully converge.

Divergent Growth

$\eta \ge 1.0$

Steps overshoot so severely that the distance from the minimum increases exponentially, leading to overflow.
